I did not know how to sew. I wanted a princess dress anyway: a structured corset bodice, a flowy skirt, the kind of thing you see in a film and think "someone made that".

## The tools first

The hot pink Singer 3223 arrived with thread in every colour, rotary cutters, scissors, chalk, needles and pins. Before I sewed a single seam, the table looked like this.

![The pink sewing machine and the first pile of supplies](/images/sewing-desk.jpg)

## The bodice

I chose a shiny teal satin and cut the first pieces of the corset bodice. I started from a commercial pattern and adjusted it by feel, so it fit snugly around the waist. Every panel was sewn, pressed and lined up. I hand-stitched where it needed precision and ironed every seam.

![The corset base with boning channels, in teal satin](/images/dress-fabric.jpg)

## The finish

The skirt is satin panels with a see-through organza overskirt, and a ruched ribbon detail cinches the waist. It was my first real project, and I treated it like a real one: not "good enough", but what I had imagined.

## What it taught me

- Learning does not need permission. Tutorials, a lot of looking up words, and trying.
- Mistakes are part of it. I resewed seams, unpicked zips and poked myself with pins.
- Your hands learn even when your head is full. Sewing got smoother because my hands remembered.

Want to try? Borrow a machine, pick a fabric you love, sew a drawstring bag or just straight lines. Mess up, then keep going.
